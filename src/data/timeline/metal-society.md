# THE METAL SOCIETY

**~1,300,000 BU · THE FIRST METAL**
> Until recently I knew nothing about the Metal Society. A copy of their archives came with the agreement Nightshade settled with them, and Seraphine read through it before passing it to me. Most of what follows comes from those archives. The oldest parts were copied over many times before the copy we have was made, and in places the copyists admit they couldn't read what they were copying.
>
> The Society began on a planet the Metals call Anvil. It sits well past anything I had mapped, and it's mostly iron, with enough heat under the surface that some of it has never cooled.
>
> A Metal woke up there alone. The archives refer to him often, but in our copy every place his name was written has been scraped away. When Seraphine asked for it, the Metal who delivered the archives refused and asked that the question not be raised again. The Metals call him the First Metal.
>
> According to the oldest pages, he spent close to ten thousand years walking the planet and recording where each kind of ore could be found.

**~1,290,000 BU · THE FIRST METALS ARE MADE**
> He made the first Metal by melting ore from a hillside and pouring it into a mold cut into the rock. She was an Iron, and he named her Hollis. The archives say she asked for a name before she asked anything else, and Metals have been named on the day they're poured ever since, usually by whoever poured them.
>
> Coppers and Tins followed, and much later Silvers. A working mold for a Gold took him nine hundred years.
>
> Making Metals this way was slow. He needed the right ore, enough heat, and a mold for each kind, and the archives suggest that for a long time there were only a few hundred Metals on Anvil.

**~1,100,000 BU · THE FIRST METAL WALKS INTO THE FIRE**
> By this point there were a few thousand Metals, and Hollis and others had learned to pour new ones themselves. The First Metal told Hollis to keep the furnaces burning, walked into the largest of them, and was not seen again.
>
> The Metals waited a long time before deciding what to do. Some wanted to let the furnace cool so they could search it, and others refused to let it go out. The second group won. Every foundry in the Society still keeps one furnace that is never allowed to go out. Metals today disagree about why. Some treat it as custom, and some believe he is still inside.

**~700,000 BU · THE FIRST TOWNS**
> The archives cover the next four hundred thousand years thinly, mostly in lists of who poured whom and how much ore each furnace used. What can be pieced together is that the Metals spread out from the first foundries and built settlements around new ones, since a Metal can only be made where there's enough heat.
>
> Each settlement elected a Mayor. After the First Metal, nobody wanted a single Metal in charge of everyone, and the Society has never had one since. Mayors served for a hundred years and could be removed earlier by a vote, which happened often. Towns traded ore and molds with one another, and molds were valuable enough that most towns kept theirs under guard.

**~500,000 BU · THE BRONZE WAR**
> Copper ore was found mostly near the copper towns, and tin ore mostly near the tin towns. Both needed the other's ore, and over time disputes over trade became open fighting. The war lasted a little over three hundred years.
>
> It was fought carefully. A Metal who melts can be poured again from the same ore, but the Metal who comes out of the mold is someone new and remembers nothing, so both sides avoided losing soldiers where they could, and much of the war was sieges.
>
> It ended when the Mayors of Verdigris and Cassiter, the largest town on each side, agreed to split the ore fields along the river between them. The treaty is still in force.

**~498,000 BU · THE ALLOY EXPERIMENTS**
> During the last years of the war, a foundry master in Verdigris named Maud melted down a Copper and a Tin together. Both were prisoners taken from Cassiter. When the furnace was opened, she poured out a Bronze, a new Metal harder than either of the two who went in. The Bronze had none of their memories and was given a name of her own the same day.
>
> Maud's records of the experiment are detailed. She noted the names and weights of both prisoners, the temperature of the furnace, and how long each of them stayed conscious after going in. The Copper was conscious for most of an hour.
>
> After the war, other foundries took up the work. Steel was made from two Irons and a material the records never name. Stellite required a Cobalt, a Chromium, and a Tungsten, and the records state that all three had to be alive when they went into the furnace for the pour to take. Each foundry kept a ledger of the Metals it used. Some entries are marked as volunteers. Most are not marked at all, and the ledgers don't say where those Metals came from.

**~150,000 BU · ENGINES**
> An Iron named Odile, working in one of the southern towns, found a way to make the heat of a furnace drive a wheel. The archives describe several earlier attempts that failed, two of them badly, but hers worked and was copied.
>
> Over the next few thousand years, engines spread through every town on Anvil. The Metals built rail lines between their towns, then factories that ran day and night, and the sky over the industrial towns turned brown and stayed that way. Most of the Society's population growth in the archives comes after this, once foundries could pour Metals far faster than by hand. This is also roughly when Mayors began wearing top hats, a custom the archives record without ever explaining.

**~20,000 AU · THE BURNING OF CINDER**
> Cinder was the largest city in the Society. An Iron named Garrick, the most respected of its engineers, built an engine twelve stories high meant to power the whole city from a single furnace.
>
> It was started on a workday, with most of the city's Metals at their posts. The furnace failed a few hours later. The fire that followed was hot enough that Metals in its path melted where they stood rather than burning, and the archives include accounts from survivors who saw it reach the factories while they were still full. Melted Metals ran together as the heat spread, and when it finally cooled, the remains could not be separated into individuals to be poured again.
>
> The fire burned for eleven years. About a third of the city's population was lost, Garrick among them. A new Cinder was built some distance away. The old city was never cleared.

**~20,011 AU · THE PATTERN LAW**
> In the year the fire went out, the towns of the Society passed the Pattern Law together. Under it, no new machine could be built unless it was a copy of one already proven to work. A Metal who wanted to build something new had to present it to a council made up of the oldest Metals in the Society, many of whom had lost others in Cinder.
>
> The council approved very few designs, and most of those were improvements to the kettles Metals use for warming oil. Over the following centuries, the Society's machines stopped changing.

**~300,000 AU · THE COUNCIL NEVER CHANGES**
> Metals don't age, and most of them only die if they're melted. Because of this, many of the Metals who wrote the Pattern Law were still on the council three hundred thousand years later.
>
> There were attempts to repeal it. The longest was led by a Steel named Ida, who presented a new engine design to the council regularly over two centuries and was refused each time. Younger Metals have never made up a majority of the council, and the archives record no attempt to change the law succeeding.

**~600,000 AU · GOLDS FOR HIRE**
> Golds were the most difficult Metals to make and among the least occupied, since their work had changed little since the first ones were poured.
>
> Over time, some of them began taking work outside their own towns, guarding rail lines and settling disputes between towns, and eventually working for whoever would pay. The council did not approve, but it had no way to stop them. Each contract was signed with the Gold's own name, and a Gold who broke one could expect other Golds to refuse to work alongside her.

**~1,000,000 AU · THE SOCIETY STANDS STILL**
> By the time Maroon found the Society, its towns, rail lines, and factories were very close to what they had been for most of a million years. The machines were well maintained and almost none of them were new.
>
> Metals who grew up in the Society tend to describe it as orderly and safe. Those who left for the Gempire more often describe it as a place where nothing was allowed to change.
