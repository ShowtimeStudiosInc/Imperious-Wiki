# ERA 1A, THE AGE OF NEW LIGHT

**0 AU - 4,000 AU · 4,847 TO 1,923**
> The golden stone sat on a shelf in my rooms for four thousand years. Every few weeks it glowed for a moment, then went back to being a stone.
>
> My gems spent most of those years arguing about it. They had just fought a war against one Diamond, and they had fought her gems up close, the ones who could move and speak but had no will of their own. When White's gems took some of my essence and slowly began to choose things for themselves, that helped, but not enough. Nobody could promise that the stone on my shelf wouldn't grow into another White, and nobody wanted to stay and find out.
>
> In the end it went to the assembly, and the assembly voted 4,847 to 1,923 to leave.
> They took everything they could carry, and they could carry a great deal: every building on Homeworld's surface, every ship, every machine that could be lifted. What they couldn't carry was the part they had built into the planet itself, so they left it behind, still running, and it ran on its own for a very long time.
>
> I found them a planet with three moons and good ground, and folded the space around it so you'd need to know exactly where to look to find it. They named it Hades 9. I'm still an aristocrat there, and I still vote when I have an opinion. I didn't go with them, though. The stone had been in my care for four thousand years, and someone had to be here when it emerged.
>
> My daughters stayed with me, and so did about forty others, all Era 0. Hester, a Ruby barely five feet tall, who had fought through the whole war. Doran, a Jasper who had run part of our intelligence work. Ilse, a Sapphire who said she wanted to see what happened next. I think most of the forty stayed out of curiosity, and some out of loyalty. I never asked which.
**4,000 AU · THAT'S RIGHT, ISN'T IT?**
> Theta Gold Diamond emerged in a canyon I had chosen because the rock there was interesting. The light that came with him was warm and gold, and it turned the canyon walls to glass for about a mile in each direction.
>
> When it faded, he was standing there, ninety feet tall, with his gemstone at his chest. He looked at the canyon, then at the empty horizon, and then down at his own hands for a long time.
>
> "Hello," I said.
> "Hello." He looked up at me. "Where am I?"
> "Homeworld, or what's left of it. I'm Alpha Black Diamond."
> "I'm Theta Gold Diamond," he said, and then, after a moment, "That's right, isn't it?"
>
> I told him it sounded right to me.
>
> Seraph walked us back to the only building I had kept, a single house with a roof, four walls, and furniture from before the war. Theta had never sat in a chair before, and it took him a few tries.
> He asked what had happened here, and I told him. A civilization had lived on this planet, and four thousand years ago it had left, because it was afraid of what he might turn out to be.
>
> "They don't know me," he said.
> "They knew the last Diamond I met."
>
> I told him about White, and her gems, and what it had been like to fight them. I left out where White was now. I didn't think he needed that on his first day.
>
> He was quiet for a while. "What if I'm like her?"
> "Then I'll deal with it," I said. "I don't think you are, though. She never once asked that question."
>
> He thought about that, then asked what we were supposed to do now. I said we could build something, and he asked whether we'd do it together. I said yes. He seemed relieved, and I was a little surprised to find that I was too.
**4,000 AU - 200,000 AU · NO TWO**
> There was very little to build with. The surface was bare rock and ice, and there were forty of us.
>
> Under that surface, the machinery my gems had built was still running. Theta didn't know it was there. The surface had been made to look like ordinary land, and I didn't tell him otherwise. The forty knew, and none of them brought it up either. When he asked me why the ground was warmer in some places than it had any right to be, I told him Homeworld was an unusual planet, which was true.
>
> In his second week he asked me how gems had been made before.
> "I pushed my essence into the ground and waited," I said.
> He looked at me for a while. "Were they all the same?"
> "No two."
> He wrote something down. "We should find a better way."
>
> He spent years studying the crust: how deep the essence needed to go, how warm the rock had to be, how long it took. I had always done it by feel. He wanted numbers, and when he couldn't get them out of me, he went and got them himself.
**200,000 AU · ALL TEN ANSWERED**
> His first gems were made the old way, by hand, but measured. Depth, temperature, and timing were all written down and repeated as closely as he could manage. The first batch was ten Peridots. All ten emerged at the same height and very nearly the same color. When Theta spoke to one of them, all ten answered.
>
> "Is that normal?" Theta asked.
> "Not for mine," I said.
>
> The Peridots turned out to be very good with machines. Within a few months they had worked out how to build holoscreens again, which Ilse had been failing at for close to two hundred thousand years. Within a few decades they had helped Theta build the first injector, a device that could take essence, store it, and place it exactly where he wanted.
>
> That was what made the empire possible. I could make a gem whenever I liked, but only one at a time, by hand. With an injector, Theta could plant a hundred at once and walk away.
**200,000 AU - 500,000 AU · WHERE THE ANIMALS GROW CRYSTAL**
> Even with injectors, there was only so much essence to go around. Every gem needs some essence to form, and two Diamonds can only produce so much. Theta could plant far more efficiently than I ever had, but he couldn't plant what we didn't have, so he started measuring that too.
>
> The first colony was founded soon after, on a nearby world with good rock and very few earthquakes. Fifty gems went out to build it.
>
> Farther out, in a galaxy the survey teams named Canopus Reach, Theta found a desert planet made almost entirely of canyons. Getting there meant folding space, which only I could do. Everything after that was Theta's work. He turned the whole planet into a kindergarten, divided it into numbered sectors, and powered it with heat drawn up from deep under the surface. At its center he built his own seat, with labs, housing, offices, and eventually a throne room. Newly emerged gems were organized there before being sent to their first colony. The planet's designation is 0g4g-22c. Most gems just call it Solstice.
>
> Some of the essence planted there soaked into the land and stayed. Out among Solstice's canyons, near the few oases, the animals have started growing crystal. Some of them can do things animals shouldn't be able to, and some of them are parasites. Theta keeps notes on all of it.
> Somewhere in all of this, Theta asked how the empire would be governed. The gems who had left had governed themselves, and I had spent my whole existence as one voice among many. This time someone had to be in charge, and there were only two of us.
>
> So we divided it. I ruled Homeworld and commanded its army as General, and I kept watch over Theta's work, as I would later keep watch over Epsilon's. Theta ran the kindergartens, the colonies, and everything at Canopus Reach. It was the first time I had ever agreed to rule anything. I still think about why I said yes.
>
> We kept the freedoms that mattered. Gems could own things, earn things, and choose their own work. We gave them structure and safety, and left the rest to them.
**500,000 AU - 800,000 AU · SEVEN OTHER ALPHAS**
> A survey team found the Haelan by accident, living in oasis cities on a desert world well past our maps. No two of them looked alike. Some had skin, some had scales or fur, and a few had shells. Their ambassador was about the size of a large jar, and the guard behind her stood nearly fifteen feet tall. None of them seemed to find us strange. A species that varied that much had never seen much point in being afraid of things that looked different.
>
> The ambassador looked up at me and said, "Va drash tu." I said hello back, which turned out to be the wrong answer. It means "I see you," and the proper reply is "Tu drash va," you see me. I've used it correctly ever since. They called us vel-thren, small lights, and as far as I can tell, they meant it kindly.
> They were led by seven of their largest, the biggest nearly eighty feet tall, and they called these leaders Alphas. That caused some confusion at the first meeting, and a great deal of paperwork afterward.
>
> We traded. They had materials we needed, and they could take other shapes, which made my Era 0 gems more comfortable among them than anywhere else since the war.
>
> Once a year, the Haelan held what they called the Sigil Awakening, and the whole species turned out for it. Theta went to one as a gesture of goodwill and came back with three pages of notes.
>
> "We should have one of those," he said.
> "We don't have a religion."
> "We don't need one. They like being in the same place. They like having a reason to get together."
> The first Festival of the First Light was held around 520,000 years into the era, on the anniversary of my emergence, as near as anyone could work it out. There were glowing crystals, a good deal of standing around, and a Grand Ball at the end, because Theta had been reading about balls. It went better than either of us expected. After that he kept finding reasons for more. The Season of the Blooming came from the Rev flowers that open on new kindergarten land. New Dawn marked the start of each production cycle, when gems reported what they'd done and what they meant to do next.
>
> By the end of the era, the Gempire had colonies in two galaxies, holidays of its own, and some sense of what it was. What it didn't have was enough essence. Theta's numbers made it plain. The empire was growing faster than two Diamonds could make gems for it.
>
> There was still a fourth stone out there somewhere, and I still didn't know where.
