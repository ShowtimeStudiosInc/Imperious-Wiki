# ERA 1B, THE AGE OF THE JUDGE

**800,000 AU - 850,000 AU · THREE THOUSAND YEARS FOR A SHUTTLE**
> By the start of this era we had courts, in the sense that on most colonies someone had been put in charge of settling arguments. Each of them made up the rules as they went.
>
> The case that finally got our attention involved two Rubies on two different colonies. Both had borrowed a shuttle without asking, and both had brought it back undamaged the same day. One was told not to do it again. The other was sent to a mining moon for three thousand years.
>
> Theta read both reports twice. "We need standards," he said. "Neither of us knows how to build them."
> "The United Frontier had a proper court system," I said. "I remember how it worked in general, but not the details."
> "Then we need someone whose purpose is exactly that."
>
> Neither of us said the rest out loud. It was also the answer to the other problem on Theta's desk, the essence shortage. Two Diamonds were no longer enough. We needed a third.
**850,000 AU - 1,050,000 AU · HESTER WAS BORED**
> About three hundred thousand years earlier, I had set up a small intelligence network to keep track of what was happening across our territory. I gave it a new job: look for anything that shouldn't exist. Large gemstones in odd places. Rock that didn't match the ground around it.
>
> Hester ran it. She had fought through the whole war, and peacetime work had never really suited her. When I explained the assignment, she said it sounded like the old days, and asked when she could start.
>
> Over the years her teams turned up a few ordinary gemstones sitting in places they had no reason to be. We brought them home and let them emerge properly. Nothing came close to the size of a Diamond until, two hundred thousand years in, a team reported back from Sector Blue-7.
**1,050,000 AU · THE READINGS DON'T MAKE SENSE**
> The planet in Sector Blue-7 was home to a people who walked on two legs, were covered in short fur, and carved stone better than anyone I have met before or since. At the center of their oldest city stood a temple, and at the center of the temple sat a blue gemstone. They had been worshipping it for fifty thousand years.
>
> Ilse ran the scans. "It's a gemstone," she reported, "but the readings don't make sense. It's older than the planet."
>
> It took her a few weeks to work out why. The stone hadn't landed there. The planet had formed around it, with the stone at its center from the very beginning. I knew what it was before I arrived. It was the last of the four.
>
> I noticed something else about the people who lived there. Their courts were older than ours, and theirs worked. Disputes were settled quickly and, as far as anyone could tell, fairly. Nobody on that world could explain why their people had always been like that. I had some idea.
**1,050,000 AU · THEY'LL MANAGE**
> I went myself, and I brought Theta, since whoever we added to the government would be working with him too.
>
> The high priest met us at the temple steps. "This is sacred," she said, through the translator. "You can't take it."
> "We're not taking it," Theta said. "We're helping it become what it's meant to be."
> "It's meant to be here."
>
> The negotiations lasted six months. In the end we left them technology, resources, and medicine well beyond anything they had, and they agreed, though nobody on that planet was happy about it.
>
> "They'll manage," Theta said as we left.
> "You don't know that."
> "They're resilient. They'll figure it out."
>
> I've never gone back to find out which of us was right.
**1,050,000 AU · THAT WAS WRONG**
> I placed the blue stone in a canyon on Homeworld, the way I had with Theta's, and expected to wait a few thousand years. It took eleven days.
>
> Epsilon Blue Diamond emerged in a burst of blue light that knocked out power across the nearest district for an afternoon. When it cleared, she was standing in the canyon, looking at each of us in turn.
>
> She asked where she was, and then she asked how she had gotten there. I told her about the temple, the priest, the six months, and what we had left behind.
>
> "That was wrong," she said. She didn't say it angrily. She said it plainly, the way you'd state any other fact.
>
> Theta started to explain our reasons: the courts, the essence, everything the empire needed. She listened to all of it. "I understand why you did it," she said. "It was still wrong."
>
> I didn't argue with her. Over the next several hundred thousand years I learned that arguing with Epsilon about right and wrong is a waste of everyone's time. She has never once been mistaken about it.
**1,050,000 AU - 1,200,000 AU · WE ARE STILL PAYING**
> Epsilon spent her first years reading every case either of us had ever heard of, and then she wrote the law. One code for the whole empire. Courts on every colony, with judges trained to apply it the same way everywhere. Penalties that matched the offense, no matter who committed it or where. The Ruby on the mining moon was brought home and given a written apology, which she framed.
>
> The first entry in the new law was a judgment against the Gempire itself. It found that the stone had been taken from the people of Sector Blue-7 without their real consent, and it ordered that they be paid for it every year, for as long as the Gempire exists. We are still paying.
>
> A third Diamond also meant a third supply of essence. The shortage eased, the kindergartens filled again, and the empire went back to growing.
**1,100,000 AU · MARKED TWICE**
> Epsilon's law worked. Disputes were settled the same way on every colony, and most gems were glad of it. Some were not. A gem who had lived under her own colony's rules for half a million years without a problem didn't always see why she needed new ones.
>
> Hester's network noticed the first signs before anyone else did. A Jasper on an outer colony left her post one night and was never found. A supply shipment went missing between two worlds that had no reason to lose it. Then the same mark turned up scratched into a wall on one colony, and a few months later on another, in a different galaxy. Nobody claimed it.
>
> It seemed like a small thing, and I treated it like one. That turned out to be a mistake. These were the first signs of S.T.O.R.M.
**1,150,000 AU · YES BEFORE I'D FINISHED ASKING**
> By then, a search network was no longer enough. I needed to know what was happening across two galaxies without being seen to ask, and I needed the United Frontier to know as much as we did, since whatever was starting would not care about the border between us.
>
> So I built the Agency, to serve the Gempire and the United Frontier both. Hester's network became the core of it. Its divisions came together over the next few thousand years: HUNTER, INTEL, ARCHIVE, FORGE, and WARD. I took the title of Director-General, which mostly means every report eventually ends up on my desk.
>
> I went to Hades 9 to ask one gem in person. Seraphine has been my closest friend since long before the war, and nobody alive knows more history than she does. I asked her to run ARCHIVE. She said yes before I'd finished asking, then made me sit through the rest of what I had planned to say anyway.
**1,200,000 AU · THE 3,900TH FLOOR**
> With Epsilon writing the law and Theta running the colonies, the empire no longer needed to see me. It only needed me to keep doing my job.
>
> By then, the house I had kept on Homeworld was no longer just a house. Over the years we had added a room for Theta, then one for Epsilon, then a meeting room for the three of us, then a library, a hangar, and a great many floors in between, most of them built underneath it. It's called the Throne of Homeworld now, and it stands three hundred and seventy miles tall. My rooms take up the whole of the 3,900th floor, built around what's left of the original house.
>
> So I stepped out of public view and went upstairs. I still ruled Homeworld, still commanded its army, and still kept watch over the other two Diamonds, and I did all of it from up there, through the Agency, my daughters, and the gems of Era 0. Apart from the other Diamonds, they are the only ones allowed up. Theta and Epsilon each asked me why, at different times. I gave them both the same answer, and neither of them found it satisfying.
>
> Most gems alive today have never seen me, and a good number of them aren't sure I exist. That suits me well enough.
