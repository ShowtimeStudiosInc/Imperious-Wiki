<span class="ia-label in-world">In-World</span>

Homeworld doesn't orbit a star. It's a rogue planet, drifting on its own through a cluster of galaxies, with no sun to light it and no air of its own. You might expect it to be dark forever. It isn't.

## The Sky

A vast holographic sphere surrounds Homeworld's city. It simulates a sky, holds in an atmosphere, and gives the city its days and nights. Two great orbs cross that sky, one standing in for a sun and the other for a moon. Both travel along the same line of longitude the Throne of Homeworld stands on, so from the Throne, the sun always passes directly overhead.

The sky can be adjusted. Once a year, on Lowlight, it is set to its darkest night of the year.

## Sector Null

The sphere's sky only covers the city. Past its edge lies Sector Null, the frozen, unlit wilderness that makes up the rest of the planet. There, the real sky shows: no sun, no moon, only distant stars. It's where Homeworld's first kindergartens were dug, long since abandoned. Scouts, rogues, and the occasional lost Gem are the only ones who go there.
