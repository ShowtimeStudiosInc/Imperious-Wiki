<span class="ia-label in-world">In-World</span>

Every Pearl begins in a Reef. A Reef is run by a Nacre, and it is three things at once: the place where Pearls are made, the school that trains them, and the home they return to when they have nowhere else to go. To the Gems who visit, a Reef looks like the finest jewelry store they will ever walk into. To the Pearls who grow up there, it's home.

## Inside a Reef

Most Reefs share the same layout, whatever their size.

- **The Bed:** The deepest, most private room, where the Nacre makes new Pearls. Only the Nacre and her most trusted senior Pearls are allowed inside.
- **The Schoolrooms:** Where Pearls are trained. A small Reef may have one. The largest have dozens, each for a different skill.
- **The Showroom:** Where Pearls are presented to visiting Gems. This is the Reef's public face, and a Nacre puts everything she has into it.
- **The Quarters:** Where Pearls who live at the Reef rest between lessons. That includes the newly made, the not-yet-chosen, and those who have come home.
- **The Ledger Room:** Where every Pearl's record is kept: when she was made, what she was trained in, who chose her, and where she is now.

## Who Runs a Reef

- **The Nacre:** Owner, maker, and to most of her Pearls, something close to a mother. Her name is the Reef's name, and her reputation is its reputation.
- **Senior Pearls:** Experienced Pearls who teach the younger ones. Many were placed once, lost their superior, and came home to teach.
- **The Ledger Pearl:** Keeps the records, the waiting list, and the appointment book. In a busy Reef, she decides who gets to see the Nacre, which makes her more powerful than she looks.
- **Reef Hands:** Pearls who were never chosen and stayed on. They keep the Reef running, greet visitors, and know more about its business than anyone admits.

## Reef Names

Officially, a Reef is known by its Nacre's name. In practice, most earn a nickname over time, and the nickname sticks. A Reef known for its glass-walled Showroom might be called the Glasshouse. A small Reef on an outer colony might be called the Low Tide. One that takes in Pearls nobody else will might be called Second Chances.

## Reef Standing

Not all Reefs are equal, and everyone knows it.

- **Crown Reefs:** The most famous Reefs in the empire. They serve the Diamonds' courts and the highest Aristocrats, keep waiting lists that run for centuries, and turn away more Gems than they see. A Pearl from a Crown Reef carries its name for life, and it opens doors.
- **Court Reefs:** Respected Reefs that serve Authoritative Gems and well-known Militia. Solid training, good names, and a real chance of rising to Crown standing, or falling.
- **Common Reefs:** Small Reefs that serve Gems who have just earned their first Pearl. Their Pearls are less polished and often more surprising.

A Reef's standing is never fixed. It rises when its Pearls serve well and falls when they cause scandal. One famous placement can lift a Common Reef overnight. One Pearl caught helping a lawbreaker can sink a Crown Reef in a decade.

## The Eras of the Reef

The oldest Reefs belong to Era 0 Nacres, near Homeworld, and their Pearls carry Alpha Black's essence. Era 1 Nacres opened their Reefs during the great expansion, and their Pearls are known for being polished and dependable. Era 2 Nacres opened their first Reefs only a year ago. Nobody knows yet what their Pearls will become, which makes them a gamble, and a bargain.

## Showings

A Gem with the right to a Pearl books an appointment with a Reef. At a Crown Reef, that can take longer than most Gems have been alive.

On the day of the Showing, the Gem is led into the Showroom, and the Nacre presents her Pearls one at a time. She tells the Gem who each Pearl is, what she has trained in, and what she is best suited for. The Gem may ask questions. By tradition, each Pearl may ask the Gem one question in return, and how a Gem answers often decides more than they realize.

A Gem may meet many Pearls before choosing, and a Pearl may be passed over many times before she is chosen. A Nacre may also refuse a Gem she believes would treat her Pearls badly. Few do, since refusing the wrong Gem can cost a Reef its standing.

## Pricing

Pearls are never bought with currency. The right to choose one comes from rank or from service:

- Aristocratic and Authoritative Gems, and every rank above them, may choose a Pearl as part of their position.
- Gems of lower ranks can earn the right through a major service to the Empire, such as leading a battle to victory, making an important discovery or invention, or saving the life of a high-ranking Gem.

Reefs themselves are kept running by patrons. A court that is pleased with its Pearls sends materials, protection, and favors back to the Reef that trained them. A Crown Reef usually has a Diamond's court as its patron.

## The School

Before a Pearl is ever presented, her Reef trains her. Every Pearl learns record-keeping, etiquette, and Imperial law, and the first lesson is always the same: her loyalty belongs to the law before anyone else.

Beyond that, each Reef teaches what it is known for. Some turn out the best archivists in the empire. Others train musicians, translators, negotiators, or bodyguards. A Pearl's training is part of who she is, and many Gems choose a Reef for its specialty before they ever meet a Pearl.

## Marks

When a Pearl is chosen, she is given her Reef's mark, a small symbol she carries for life, worn as a pin, a pattern on her clothing, or a design on her form. Other Pearls can tell at a glance where she came from. So can anyone who knows what to look for.

## Coming Home

Once a year, placed Pearls are allowed a visit home, to see their Nacre and be repaired if they need it. These visits are when news travels. Pearls from a dozen courts share the same Quarters for a night, and a careful Nacre learns more about the empire in one week than most Aristocrats learn in a century.

## The Orphanage

A Pearl whose superior is shattered or disgraced goes home to her Reef. So does a Pearl who has never been chosen. Some are presented again and find a new place. Some never do, and stay on as Reef Hands or teachers. Pearls who come home are called Returned, and a Reef's Returned are a quiet measure of how well it has placed its Pearls.

## Rules of the Reef

- No Gem may have more than two Pearls, and having two is very rare. The Diamonds are the only exception.
- A Pearl cannot have a Pearl of her own.
- Mistreating a Pearl is a crime under Imperial law. A Nacre who can prove it may call her Pearl home.
- A Pearl's first loyalty is to Imperial law, not to the Gem she serves. If her superior breaks the law, she is expected to report it when she safely can. A Pearl who sides with a lawbreaking superior against the law has broken it herself.

## Playing at the Reef

<span class="ia-label">Rules · out of character</span>

This part covers how Reef roleplay works in practice: which roles you can take, what needs staff approval, and how Showings and placements are run.

### Roles

- **Nacre:** Runs a Reef. Requires staff approval, and each Nacre player may run one Reef.
- **Reef Pearl:** A Pearl who lives at a Reef and hasn't been placed yet. Can take on a Reef job: teacher, Ledger Pearl, or Reef Hand.
- **Placed Pearl:** A Pearl who serves a Gem. Keeps her Reef's mark and can still visit home.
- **Client:** Any Gem with the right to choose a Pearl.

### Opening a Reef

To open a Reef, a Nacre player submits its name, location, specialty, and Era to staff. Every new Reef starts at Common standing. A Nacre can fill her Reef with NPC Pearls, as long as they stay in the background and don't overshadow player characters.

### Running a Showing

1. The Client posts a request to visit the Reef, and states how they earned the right to a Pearl: their rank, or a service staff have already approved.
2. The Nacre player hosts the Showing, presenting NPC Pearls, player Pearls, or both.
3. Each Pearl may ask the Client one question.
4. The Client chooses, or leaves without choosing.
5. If a Pearl is chosen, post the placement, so staff can log it.

### Placement Between Players

If a player's Pearl is chosen by another player's Gem, **both players must agree out of character first.** Nobody can force a placement onto another player's character, and nobody can refuse one on behalf of another player.

### Reef Standing in Play

Staff decide when a Reef rises or falls, based on what happens in roleplay: famous placements, scandals, Pearls who serve well or badly. Changes are announced in the Reef channel.

### What You Can't Do

- Buy or sell a Pearl with currency
- Choose a Pearl without having earned the right
- Hold more than two Pearls, unless you play a Diamond
- Give a Pearl a Pearl of her own
- Use NPC Pearls to win arguments, scenes, or fights for you

### Sensitive Scenes

Storylines involving a Pearl being mistreated need both players' agreement out of character before they start, and either player can end them at any time. If a Nacre wants to call a Pearl home for mistreatment, staff decide the outcome based on the roleplay.

### Reporting Your Superior

A player Pearl decides for herself whether to report a superior who breaks the law. Choosing not to is allowed, but it has consequences in roleplay if it comes out.
